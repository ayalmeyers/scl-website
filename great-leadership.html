Finding the Right Mix: Leadership vs. "Executiveship"

SCL Musings

Modern organizations have overemphasized execution at the expense of genuine leadership. As the saying nearly attributed to Napoleon goes: becoming a great statesman requires compromise and focus on immediate tasks, while being one demands vision and moral strength. Execution appears to have come at the expense of leadership.

Executives focus on accomplishment and results. They drive efficiency and high standards — but left unchecked, this orientation can foster cultures of unhealthy competition and a survival-of-the-fittest mentality.

Leaders envision and inspire. They articulate compelling visions and enroll others through alignment rather than coercion. But this approach, unchecked, can breed autocracy or impractical idealism.

Neither the ethos of an executive nor a leader is enough alone. It is the balance of both that makes a high-performing person, or team, great. The remedy is not to pick a side — it is to identify and develop people who can hold both orientations at once: driving results without losing sight of vision, and inspiring others without losing grip on execution.
